Run a Small-Business AI Policy Workshop

A small business does not need a hundred-page AI policy. It needs clear, usable decisions about approved tools, protected information, human review, ownership, and what to do when the answer is uncertain.

Start here

How to use this resource

Policies often fail because they are written far from the work. Employees receive broad prohibitions or vague encouragement, but no practical guidance for the situations they actually encounter.

The Coffee Cup AI governance and adoption guidance connects governance to workflow and employee adoption. This workshop format helps a small organization create a short first policy and a process for improving it as tools and uses change.

01

Map the uses already happening

Invite participants from several roles to name work-related AI uses they have observed or attempted. Group them by task, information handled, audience, and consequence rather than by product name.

The map gives the policy a factual starting point. It also reveals where employees need an approved alternative instead of a rule they cannot apply.

  • Use case and business purpose
  • Information and audience
  • Consequence if the output is wrong
02

Set clear traffic-light boundaries

Define green uses that are generally allowed, yellow uses that require approval or extra review, and red uses that are prohibited. Provide role-specific examples and a contact for questions.

Keep the categories tied to information and consequence so they remain useful when vendors change their features.

  • Green: low-risk approved uses
  • Yellow: approval or enhanced review
  • Red: prohibited information or decisions
03

Define human accountability

State who verifies facts, approves customer-facing outputs, authorizes tools, responds to incidents, and maintains the policy. A policy without owners becomes a suggestion.

Add a simple escalation path for uncertain requests. Employees should know how to pause and ask without being penalized for caution.

  • Output reviewer
  • Tool and data approver
  • Incident and policy owner
04

Publish a living one-page policy

Write the first version in plain language with examples, a question channel, and the date it will be reviewed. Link to supporting procedures for teams that need more detail.

Schedule a short recurring review using employee questions, new uses, incidents, and tool changes as evidence. The policy becomes a maintained operating guide rather than a document forgotten at launch.

  • One-page core policy
  • Examples and question channel
  • Owner, version, and review date

Questions teams ask

Questions and answers

Should a small business ban all unapproved tools?

The organization should set boundaries that match its information and risk, while providing clear approved options and a practical request path.

Does a policy replace legal or security advice?

No. A practical operating policy should be reviewed with the appropriate legal, security, privacy, or compliance professionals for the business.

How often should the policy change?

Review it on a regular cadence and whenever a material tool, use, incident, contract, or regulatory obligation changes.

Operating takeaway

A short policy works when employees helped ground it in real work, owners are named, and questions continuously improve the guidance.
